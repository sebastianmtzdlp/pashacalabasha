Warm vintage tones on a budget: why we love Kodak ColorPlus 200

Kodak's cheapest roll has family-album yellows, visible grain and a character no expensive film can fake.

By Pasha CalabashaOctober 23, 20262 min read

KODAK 200-8ISO 200

Some rolls are bought for what they promise, and some are bought because they were the ones on the shelf. ColorPlus 200 has always been the second kind: the one in the yellow box next to the batteries, the one that went into the camera for the holidays without much thought.

That is why it looks the way it does. It is an emulsion made for simple cameras and one-hour labs, and that left it with a warm cast, gentle contrast and grain that doesn't hide. What used to be "normal" is now exactly the look people try to fake with filters.

Where it comes from

According to PetaPixel, ColorPlus is a cousin of Kodak Gold 200: both descend from the Kodacolor VR films of the early eighties. Gold kept evolving, especially in North America; ColorPlus stayed almost frozen in time and sold more in Asia. The name didn't even exist at first. In 2025 Kodak released a Kodacolor 200 that many believe is the same film in a new box.

Price is part of its character too: per the same review, it costs a little over half of a roll of Portra. That means almost two rolls for the price of one.

What used to be "normal" is now exactly the look everyone tries to copy.

A workhorse roll

ColorPlus is a direct descendant of Kodak's consumer negatives from the eighties and nineties. Its edge still gives it away: it doesn't say "ColorPlus" but a sober KODAK 200-8.

It is only made in 35mm and has always sat at the lower end of the range. It's the roll to spend without guilt, to test a camera you just bought, or to hand to someone who has never shot film.

What it looks like

Warm, but soft. Colours are more muted than Ektar or digital, with an eighties feel: reds and pinks stand out a little more than blues and greens. It doesn't jump off the sheet; it settles in.

Contrast is low and the shadows don't close up as much. What would be drama in Ektar is nuance here. And the grain is fine: it's surprising how little you see for a consumer roll.

How to expose it

Many people shoot it at ISO 100, and it handles that extra stop well. Overexposed by two or three stops it still gives clean images; underexposed, don't go below one stop. When in doubt, give it more light.

It shines in full sun and open shade. At night or indoors without flash it runs short: that's when you want another roll.

What we like

  • Warm, soft colour with an eighties feel
  • Fine grain and surprising detail
  • Very cheap: a little over half the price of Portra

Not so much

  • Less vivid colour than Ektar or Portra
  • Low contrast: it doesn't dramatise
  • Only made in 35mm

Who is it for?

For anyone starting with film, for holidays and full-sun trips, and for anyone who wants photos that look like they came out of a shoebox.

Verdict

ColorPlus 200 doesn't try to be Portra, and that's fine. It's warm, imperfect and honest, and in Keepr it's one of the fastest ways to give a digital session the nostalgia of a roll developed at the corner lab.
