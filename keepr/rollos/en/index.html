Keepr · Rolls

Rolls. One by one.

Reviews of the films that come with Keepr: where they come from, how they look, how to expose them and what they are good for. Each one shows the same Coatepec roll developed on that film.

C-41 · ISO 200

Warm vintage tones on a budget: why we love Kodak ColorPlus 200

Kodak's cheapest roll has family-album yellows, visible grain and a character no expensive film can fake.

C-41 · ISO 200

Kodak Gold 200: a summer afternoon, rolled up

ColorPlus's slightly finer cousin. Golden yellows, warm skin, and the feeling that it's always six in the evening.

C-41 · ISO 400

Kodak UltraMax 400: the all-terrain roll with colour to spare

A stop faster than Gold and much more saturated. The roll that lasts a whole day, from midday sun to late-afternoon shade.

C-41 · ISO 400

Kodak Portra 400: the film everyone wants to copy

Soft skin, open shadows and latitude that forgives almost anything. That's why it's the favourite for weddings and portraits.

C-41 · ISO 100

Kodak Ektar 100: the negative that wanted to be a slide

The finest grain of any colour negative and a saturation that recalls Velvia, with the latitude of a negative.

C-41 · ISO 200

Fujifilm C200: the fresh answer to Kodak's warm rolls

Fresh greens, clean skies and a cool palette that comes as a relief after so much yellow.

C-41 · ISO 400

Fujifilm Superia 400: the look of two-thousands snapshots

The Fuji roll everyone carried in their bag. Fresh colour, good contrast, and it behaves well under artificial light.

C-41 · ISO 400

Fujifilm Pro 400H: the pastel that left too soon

Mint greens, airy highlights and the fourth colour layer that made it unique. It's no longer made. In Keepr it lives on.

E-6 · ISO 50

Fujifilm Velvia 50: colour with no apologies

The slide that changed landscape photography. Dense skies, electric greens and no margin for error.

C-41 · ISO 800

CineStill 800T: the night with red halos

Kodak cinema film without its protective layer, balanced for tungsten light. City lights glow with an unmistakable halo.

B&W · ISO 400

Kodak Tri-X 400: seventy years of street and documentary

The black-and-white of photojournalists. Punchy contrast and a grain that is part of photography's history.

B&W · ISO 400

Ilford HP5 Plus: black and white that takes a push

Ilford's answer to Tri-X. Softer in contrast, just as noble, and famous for handling three stops of push.

Keepr ships with more than 170 films. We'll keep adding reviews.
