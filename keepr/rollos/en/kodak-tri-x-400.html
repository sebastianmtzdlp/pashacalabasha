Kodak Tri-X 400: seventy years of street and documentary

The black-and-white of photojournalists. Punchy contrast and a grain that is part of photography's history.

By Pasha CalabashaOctober 23, 20262 min read

KODAK 400TXISO 400

Tri-X came out in the fifties and has been in the cameras of many street and press photographers ever since. If you picture a documentary black-and-white photo, you're probably picturing it in Tri-X.

What it looks like

Deep blacks, breathing whites and midtones with plenty of texture. The grain is visible and organic, never digital.

If you picture a documentary photo, you probably picture it in Tri-X.

How to expose it

At 400 it's generous; pushed to 1600 it turns harsh and grainy, which is exactly what many people want.

What we like

  • Classic character
  • Pushes very well
  • Wide latitude

Not so much

  • Strong grain for anyone wanting cleanliness

Who is it for?

For street, documentary, portraits with character and pushed night shooting.

Verdict

Black and white par excellence. In Keepr it's the most direct way to give a series weight.
