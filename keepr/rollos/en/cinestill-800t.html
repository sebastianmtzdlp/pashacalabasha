CineStill 800T: the night with red halos

Kodak cinema film without its protective layer, balanced for tungsten light. City lights glow with an unmistakable halo.

By Pasha CalabashaOctober 23, 20262 min read

CINESTILL 800TISO 800

CineStill takes Kodak cinema film, removes the black remjet layer and sells it to develop in standard C-41. Without that layer, light bounces inside the film and leaves a red halo around bright sources.

What it looks like

Cool in daylight, perfect under artificial light. Neon signs, street lamps and traffic lights get the red glow that became its signature.

A red halo that became its signature.

How to expose it

At ISO 800 at night with artificial light. In daylight it needs an 85 orange filter or it turns blue.

What we like

  • Unique halation
  • Excellent at night
  • Cinema look

Not so much

  • Blue in daylight without a filter
  • Expensive

Who is it for?

For night in the city, neon, concerts and interiors with stage lights.

Verdict

There's nothing else quite like it. In Keepr the halation is applied around the lights in each photo.
