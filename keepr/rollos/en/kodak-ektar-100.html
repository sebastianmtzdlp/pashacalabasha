Kodak Ektar 100: the negative that wanted to be a slide

The finest grain of any colour negative and a saturation that recalls Velvia, with the latitude of a negative.

By Pasha CalabashaOctober 23, 20262 min read

KODAK EKTAR 100ISO 100

Kodak sold it as the finest-grained colour negative in the world, and it is still hard to find a rival for sharpness. Where Portra softens, Ektar defines.

What it looks like

Clean, intense reds and blues, strong greens and high contrast for a negative. Skies come out deep.

It is demanding with skin: it tends to redden it. That's why it's more a landscape and city film than a portrait film.

Where Portra softens, Ektar defines.

How to expose it

Exact exposure or half a stop more. It doesn't like underexposure: shadows go blue very quickly.

What we like

  • Very fine grain
  • Vivid, clean colour
  • Great sharpness for enlargements

Not so much

  • Reddish skin
  • Needs precise exposure

Who is it for?

For landscape, architecture and travel in good light.

Verdict

If you want colour without grain, this is the answer. In Keepr it gives landscapes a very clean punch.
