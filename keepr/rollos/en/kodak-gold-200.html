Kodak Gold 200: a summer afternoon, rolled up

ColorPlus's slightly finer cousin. Golden yellows, warm skin, and the feeling that it's always six in the evening.

By Pasha CalabashaOctober 23, 20262 min read

KODAK GB 200-9ISO 200

If ColorPlus is the workhorse, Gold 200 is the Sunday roll. It shares the warm character of Kodak's consumer negatives, but with finer grain and slightly cleaner colour.

It comes in 35mm and 120, which gives it a second life among medium-format shooters who don't want to pay for Portra.

What it looks like

Golden, just as the box says. Yellows and oranges lead, greens come out warm and the skies settle into a soft blue. In afternoon light skin looks healthy and tanned.

It has a bit more saturation and contrast than Portra, but much less bite than Ektar. It's a middle ground that is very easy to like.

Gold 200 makes everything look like six in the evening.

How to expose it

Half a stop or a full stop over suits it well. It tolerates overexposure much better than underexposure, like almost all negatives.

What we like

  • Recognisably golden warmth
  • Fine grain for its price
  • Available in 120 too

Not so much

  • Can turn too yellow in cold light
  • Slightly closed shadows

Who is it for?

For low-sun landscapes, trips and casual outdoor portraits.

Verdict

This is the roll we'd recommend for a first "good" roll. In Keepr it gives an even gold that works as well on the beach as on the street.
