Kodak Portra 400: the film everyone wants to copy

Soft skin, open shadows and latitude that forgives almost anything. That's why it's the favourite for weddings and portraits.

By Pasha CalabashaOctober 23, 20262 min read

KODAK PORTRA 400ISO 400

Portra was born as a professional portrait film. You can see it in every frame: skin comes first.

Today's Portra 400 combines the best of the old NC and VC emulsions in one roll that does almost everything well. It's the film people copy most with presets, and almost nobody gets it right. The tone can be copied. What's hard is how the light and shadows behave.

What it looks like

Warm, but restrained. Skin comes out pink and smooth. Greens shift a little yellow and blues calm down. Contrast is low and saturation is just right.

Its trick is latitude. Overexpose two or three stops and the highlights stay, just more pastel.

The hard part to copy isn't the tone. It's how the light behaves.

How to expose it

Meter for the shadows and don't be afraid of light. At ISO 200 it looks even softer. Pushed to 800 it's still usable.

What we like

  • Beautiful skin tones
  • Huge latitude
  • Fine grain at ISO 400

Not so much

  • Expensive
  • Low contrast if you want punch

Who is it for?

For portraits, weddings, family, and any session where skin matters most.

Verdict

It's the reference. In Keepr it's the starting point for natural-light portraits.
